## Full and filtered policy

A complete normalized request gives every converted nonempty result an observed call, either original or reconstructed. Native mapping facts and the existing pending-only observation overlay therefore cover those IDs. Empty IDs remain unobserved and allocate on first actual conversion against the entire observed namespace, including later pending holders. One empty mapping is retained separately. Names do not change Mistral IDs; nine alphanumeric characters pass through despite an existing use of the string.

`withMistralSelection(store, slot, indices, consume)` handles fresh filtered requests. Excluding a call can make its result unobserved, and excluding the first collision holder changes a later salt. The writer persists a new scoped mapping and used namespace in the journal, observing selected calls first and allocating remaining IDs in actual conversion order, including messages later dropped. It does not reuse the full native mapping for that selection.

The supported selection is an increasing subset of the existing normalized request's unit indices. It is not a raw-row filter followed by another normalization, a reordered stream or a modified unit stream. The frozen differential covers one deterministic filtered subset per input, not every possible subset. There are zero exclusions in that tested domain. Full-request resolution rejects an unexpected unobserved nonempty ID; selected resolution rejects IDs absent from its scope. These refusals do not establish conversion outside the admitted domain.

Cold scope fill reads selected unit headers/ID pieces twice, with the existing byte-capped fact writer and trie caches. It never accumulates a historical request ID map, scans history per lookup or creates an operation file. Warm resolution uses scope admission and trie lookups. The caller supplies the retained selection indices. Distinct selections cost persistent journal space until a native fact rebuild discards their scopes. The scope version and native conversion generation are explicit; row mutations, including same-ID purges, invalidate scope identity. Clone, compaction and reopen preserve valid scopes without another fill.
